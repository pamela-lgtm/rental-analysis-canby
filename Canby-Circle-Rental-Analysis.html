Recognize the pool. Keep the price competitive.

We recommend launching at $2,795 per month once the home is vacant and show-ready. The private pool is a meaningful advantage over comparable homes without one. Our starting price recognizes that benefit while staying close to the completed leases that provide the strongest evidence of what renters will pay.

Two similar four-bedroom homes without pools leased at $2,650 and $2,700. A somewhat larger three-bedroom pool home leased at $2,700 with pool maintenance included. Together, these results support a measured premium for Canby, rather than a large jump above the completed rental market.

Our working rental range is approximately $2,695–$2,850. This is our judgment after weighing the differences, not a range of confirmed leases for identical homes. At $2,795, we are balancing the pool’s appeal with the goal of limiting vacancy. We would not recommend starting at $2,995 based on this evidence.

THE MARKET GUIDES THE OUTCOME

Price is a starting decision. Response is the test.

The market determines achievable rent, not the property management company. We review activity at day five and reassess by days 10–14. Our goal is a signed lease within 21 days of a complete marketing launch; it is not a guarantee. Effective income after vacancy matters more than the advertised monthly number.

01The property and rental offering+

The strongest features

Canby offers four bedrooms, two full bathrooms and 2,188 square feet in Temple Grove Estates. Its private pool differentiates it from the two closest four-bedroom leased comparisons. The size is also closely aligned with those homes, avoiding a major square-footage adjustment.

The recommendation assumes a vacant, fully show-ready home and routine professional pool maintenance included in the $2,795 rent. This keeps the offering comparable to the completed pool-home lease. Pool service is an owner operating expense; the rent premium is not all additional net income. If service is charged separately, we should reassess the total cost to the renter before launch.

We have not added premiums for a garage, screened enclosure, fencing, extensive remodeling or community amenities that are not established in this analysis. The private pool itself receives positive consideration.

This very similar four-bedroom, 2.5-bath home started at $2,750 on June 10, reduced to $2,650 on June 18, went pending July 29 and leased August 1. 1924 Rachels Ridge Loop: the pool benchmark

This home leased for $2,700 with pool maintenance included. Its current rental record lists three bedrooms and two baths, 2,300 square feet, a two-car garage, an oversized fenced lot, solar panels and an outdoor kitchenette. Those features compete with Canby’s additional listed bedroom.

The history shows several pending and back-on-market periods between June 14 and the September 4 lease. Its 32 CDOM therefore should not be read as a simple 32-day path from listing to rent collection; the reported listing-to-lease interval was 82 days. Earlier sales records show four bedrooms, so we give Canby’s apparent bedroom advantage limited weight rather than a large automatic adjustment.

1710 Vista Meadows Drive: useful secondary evidence

This 2017 home leased at its $2,500 asking price after 45 days. Its marketing describes remodeling, new flooring, a corner lot, flex room, loft, gated setting and included grounds care. Canby offers a fourth bedroom and private pool, but Vista Meadows has an extra half bathroom, 110 more square feet and a substantially newer, refreshed presentation. It is a useful lower-price alternative, not an equal-weight pool-home comparison.

1007 Byerly Way: not a clean whole-home comparison

This Orlando 32818 home has a private pool and four listed bedrooms, but is 382 square feet smaller than Canby. The garage is expressly excluded from the rental. Marketing says utilities are included, while the structured rent-inclusions field says none. A separate recent zero-bedroom rental record at the address further complicates the comparison without establishing how the property is currently used.

It has remained at $2,800 for 65 days. We exclude it from the primary value calculation because its location, rental configuration and inclusions differ. Its prolonged availability is a caution against assuming any pool home will lease promptly near $2,800; it is not proof that Canby cannot.

How the evidence leads to $2,795

The Cambridge leases establish a $2,650–$2,700 baseline for similarly sized four-bedroom homes without pools. Canby’s pool supports moving above that baseline, while its lack of a half bath and the need to compete on condition limit the amount of the increase.

The $2,700 Rachels Ridge pool lease provides a second check. Canby has a fourth bedroom in the current rental comparison, but less space and no established advantage over that home’s solar, fenced lot and outdoor kitchen features. This supports a modest premium, not a leap toward $3,000.

Our $2,795 recommendation is $95 above the $2,700 results and $145 above the $2,650 result. These are net differences after considering the full package—not a claim that the pool alone is worth $95–$145. We use qualitative adjustments because these five records cannot reliably price each feature separately.

The four leased properties average approximately $2,638, but that mixes three- and four-bedroom homes, pool and non-pool properties, and different service packages. We do not use that simple average as Canby’s asking rent. Similarly, leasing at the final asking price does not mean a home never needed a reduction: 1666 Cambridge reduced by $100 before leasing.

05Effective income: the cost of waiting+

Another $100 needs to pay for the wait

At $2,895 instead of $2,795, the extra rent is $1,200 over twelve fully paid months. Daily gross rent at $2,895 is approximately $95.18. Just 13 additional vacant days would consume more than the entire annual premium.

Why an early adjustment can preserve income

Moving from $2,795 to $2,695 lowers annual gross rent by $1,200. At $2,795, one vacant day represents approximately $91.89 in potential rent. About 13 extra vacant days consume that annual difference. If a $100 adjustment meaningfully shortens the wait, the lower monthly rent can produce a better income outcome.

Effective income is the rent actually earned over a defined period after vacancy and concessions. Pool service, maintenance, management fees and other operating expenses are deducted separately to evaluate net income. A vacant day cannot be rented retroactively; higher future rent only offsets the loss if it actually earns back more than was forgone.
